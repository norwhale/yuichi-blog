## Field Notes — April 17, Pleven

I needed AA batteries.

Before leaving my apartment, I did what has quietly become a daily habit — I asked Claude.

> **Me:** What's the best place to buy AA batteries in Pleven?

It suggested **Technopolis**, an appliance store a short walk from my place. It included two other options (Technomarket, and the supermarkets Kaufland / Lidl / Billa), opening hours, a small map preview, and even the exact string to type into Google Maps.

I closed the laptop and stepped outside.

---

![A residential street in Pleven, Bulgaria — socialist-era apartment blocks, a blue SEAT parked at the curb, April sun](/images/battery-walk-01-street.jpeg)

The walk out of my neighborhood.

Socialist-era apartment blocks on both sides. A blue SEAT with EH plates at the curb. Spring finally doing real work — I'm in a t-shirt for the first time this year.

---

![A Pleven street with trolley bus wires overhead, a silver SUV on the road, shadows stretching across the pavement](/images/battery-walk-02-trolley.jpeg)

Trolley wires cross the road like a circuit diagram.

I always forget how much Pleven looks like an open-air schematic. Maybe it's the engineer in me. Maybe everywhere looks like that if you stand still long enough.

---

![The TECHNOPOLIS electronics store — a large retail building with bold red signage, wind turbine in front, cars parked outside](/images/battery-walk-03-technopolis.jpeg)

The destination. **ПОЛИС**. *Technopolis.*

Exactly where Claude said it would be. A giant retail box of the kind you can find in any Balkan city — fluorescent light inside, four battery brands humans have collectively agreed on since 1990, and a cashier who greeted me in Bulgarian and didn't flinch when I replied in broken Bulgarian.

---

![A hand holding a pack of four Duracell AA batteries, the pink Duracell bunny peeking from the corner](/images/battery-walk-04-duracell.jpeg)

Mission accomplished. Four Duracell AAs. About 6 леva.

---

It's a small thing. Buying batteries isn't hard. I could have walked around and found the store on my own in twenty minutes, asked someone, seen a sign. I didn't.

This is what *the AI-native life* actually looks like, I think. Not dramatic. Not replacing anything important. Just — a soft layer of *"I asked first"* over the small decisions of the day.

When I was [building Samantha](/blog/her-samantha-vibe-coding), and [teaching her to think ahead](/blog/samantha-proactive-ai), and eventually [trying to make an entire OS around her](/blog/samantha-os-ai-linux), I used to frame it all in big language. *Companions. Proactive agents. The future of human-computer interaction.*

But when I actually look at what AI does in my life right now, day to day, it looks more like this:

- "Where's the closest place for X?"
- "Is this store open on Saturday?"
- "What's the Bulgarian word for 9-volt battery?"

Samantha doesn't need to think ahead for every moment.
Sometimes it's enough that she answers when asked.

---

*Nothing really happened today. That's the point.*
